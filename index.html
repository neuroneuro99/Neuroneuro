What we know · What we don't know

Science also
lives in questions.

Every claim on neuroneuro carries an honesty status. “We don't know yet” does not mean “we know nothing” — it marks where active research begins.

WELL ESTABLISHED — strongly supported by evidence ACTIVE RESEARCH — being studied right now DEBATED — competing interpretations exist OPEN QUESTION — no accepted answer yet
WHAT WE KNOW

Memory, as far as evidence goes.

  • The hippocampus is critical for forming new declarative memories.
  • Memory depends on changes in synaptic connections — including long-term potentiation (LTP).
  • Sleep supports the consolidation of new memories.
WHAT WE DON'T KNOW

What remains a mystery.

  • How does the brain select which memories survive for decades?
  • Why do some memories become unusually persistent?
  • How faithfully can an original memory ever be retrieved — if at all?
  • How does the brain bind fragments of experience into one whole memory?

About the project

A learning project
built around questions.

neuroneuro is an independent educational project exploring neuroscience through scientific communication, interactive learning and research-inspired experiments.

01 / Principles

Science before certainty.

Explain established findings clearly, distinguish evidence from hypotheses and make scientific limitations visible.

02 / Direction

Learning in public.

Document the process of studying brain and cognitive sciences, developing tools and improving scientific communication.
